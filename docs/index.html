PROJECT OVERVIEW

Tituli Concordienses is an open, ongoing corpus of born-digital critical editions of the inscriptions from the Eastern Necropolis (Sepolcreto dei Militi) of the ancient city of Iulia Concordia (modern-day Concordia Sagittaria, Italy), dating from the late second or third to the early fifth century AD.

The project originated as an MA thesis in Digital and Public Humanities at Ca' Foscari University of Venice by Leonardo Battistella, and is now being expanded and updated. The corpus currently includes 57 inscriptions.

The inscriptions are encoded in XML files according to the TEI/EpiDoc standard, with structured metadata, interpretive transcriptions, critical apparatus, prosopographical data, and scholarly commentaries.

Each record is rendered as an HTML page, automatically generated from its XML source file through XSLT transformations applied with Python and the saxonche library.

Each entry provides the archaeological and historical context of the inscription, an interpretive transcription with Krummrey-Panciera diacritical marks, an English translation, prosopographical data, and palaeographic or historical commentaries.

ENTRIES

Epigraphic records are divided into ten main sections, broadly following the order of the Supplementa Italica, nuova serie, as shown in the following table:

Section Description
Metadata Grid A grid containing the most relevant data: the current location and external resources, with links to other databases and projects.
Inscription Details Further details on the inscription, including findspot and place of origin (which are the same for all sarcophagi from the Eastern Necropolis), chronology with dating criteria, and autopsy information.
Physical Description Monument type, material, dimensions, and a palaeographic commentary.
Image A zoomable photograph of the inscription.
Transcription An interpretive transcription with Krummrey-Panciera diacritical marks [Krummrey-Panciera conventions], generated from XML files encoded according to the TEI/EpiDoc standard [EpiDoc Guidelines]. EpiDoc elements are converted into the corresponding diacritical marks by a custom XSLT stylesheet.
Apparatus Criticus The critical apparatus follows the standard of the Supplementa Italica, nuova serie [SupplIt]. Variant readings are listed by line number, in majuscule, similarly to a diplomatic edition.
Translation An English translation, as faithful as possible to the original text.
Commentary Historical, linguistic, palaeographic, and prosopographic context, including relationships with other inscriptions.
People Prosopographic records of the individuals mentioned in the inscription.
Bibliography Previous editions of the inscription, listed as short citations. Each short citation links to the Bibliography page, where the full reference is provided.

PROSOPOGRAPHIC RECORDS

The People page hosts records of the individuals mentioned in the inscriptions.

Information such as the onomastic series, origin of the name, gender, and occupation is provided. If the individual was a soldier, his rank and military contingent (numerus) are also reported, where attested.

The following terms are used for the roles in the prosopographic records, according to what is written in the inscriptions:

  • deceased: the individual was buried in the sarcophagus.
  • dedicator: the individual commissioned the sarcophagus for someone else.
  • dedicator/deceased: the individual commissioned their own sarcophagus while still alive. There can be more than one dedicator, especially in the case of a double burial.
  • dedicator & deceased: the individual dedicated a sarcophagus to someone else, and was later buried in another one.
  • dedicator (restoration): the individual restored an already existing sarcophagus.
  • mentioned: the individual is mentioned in the inscription but did not play any role in the burial.

Particular attention was also given to the relationships between individuals. Each record states any relationship, with a link to the corresponding prosopographic record.

OPEN DATA, CITATION & REUSE

The TEI/EpiDoc files, the bibliography, and the scripts that generate this website are openly available on GitHub and archived on Zenodo, where each release is preserved with its own persistent identifier.
